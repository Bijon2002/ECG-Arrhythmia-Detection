% ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
Automated electrocardiogram (ECG) arrhythmia classification is vital for scalable, continuous cardiac telemetry and ambulatory Holter monitoring. However, a significant fraction of existing deep learning literature reports artificially inflated diagnostic benchmarks by evaluating models on beat-wise random train-test partitions. Such intra-patient splits leak unique morphological patient signatures between training and evaluation cohorts, masking catastrophic failure modes on unseen subjects. In this work, we propose a clinically-grounded, calibrated deep ensemble architecture evaluated under the strict inter-patient DS1/DS2 partitioning benchmark established by de Chazal et al.\ on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. Our framework integrates five independently initialized 1D Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs) combining raw bandpass-filtered lead-II ECG waveforms with four domain-engineered rhythm timing features (pre-beat RR, post-beat RR, local 10-beat context RR, and prematurity ratio) optimized via class-weighted cross-entropy loss. On 49,687 heartbeats from 22 completely unseen patients (DS2), the proposed ensemble delivers an overall accuracy of 85.86\% and a macro-averaged F1-score of 0.371, significantly outperforming unweighted and single-model baselines. To eliminate overconfidence in minority arrhythmia classes, per-model post-hoc temperature scaling is introduced, reducing the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 4.28\% to 3.04\%. Furthermore, we present an uncertainty-driven selective classification paradigm benchmarking four distinct uncertainty formulations, including a novel Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) metric tailored to clinical risk tiers. By deferring the top 10\% most uncertain heartbeat predictions to cardiologists, the diagnostic accuracy on retained beats escalates from 85.86\% to 89.6\%, rising to 94.7\% at 30\% referral. This provides an actionable human-in-the-loop diagnostic framework that bridges the gap between deep learning and clinical safety.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading contributor to global morbidity and mortality, claiming an estimated 17.9 million lives each year according to the World Health Organization \cite{b1}. Among CVD manifestations, cardiac arrhythmias---ranging from benign premature ventricular contractions to life-threatening ventricular tachycardias and fibrillations---pose severe clinical challenges. Ambulatory electrocardiography (e.g., 24- to 48-hour Holter monitoring and continuous telemetry) has become indispensable for capturing intermittent arrhythmic events. However, a single 24-hour recording yields over 100,000 heartbeats. Manual beat-by-beat annotation by expert electrophysiologists is labor-intensive, costly, mentally fatiguing, and prone to diagnostic oversight.

Consequently, automated computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) systems for ECG rhythm interpretation have been extensively investigated. In recent years, deep learning models, particularly 1D Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs), have emerged as the dominant paradigm due to their automated feature learning capabilities directly from raw digitized physiological potentials \cite{b4,b5}.

Despite numerous publications reporting classification accuracies exceeding 98\% on the standard MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database \cite{b2}, a critical methodological discrepancy impairs translation into real-world hospital workflows: \emph{intra-patient data leakage} \cite{b3,b12,b13}. Conventional evaluations randomly shuffle segmented heartbeats across training and test splits. Because individual patients possess idiosyncratic cardiac conduction morphologies (e.g., patient-specific baseline voltage, ST elevation patterns, and QRS vector orientations), random beat-wise partitioning places identical wave templates from the same subject into both training and test sets. The model effectively memorizes subject identity rather than learning generalized pathological signatures. When tested on unfamiliar patients in actual clinical practice, such systems suffer catastrophic performance collapse.

To enforce clinical validity, de Chazal et al.\ \cite{b3} formalized the inter-patient DS1/DS2 evaluation standard adhering to the Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) EC57 guidelines \cite{b6}. Under this protocol, patients are strictly segregated: 22 records form the training set (DS1), and 22 entirely independent subjects constitute the test cohort (DS2). Under this benchmark, published model performance routinely drops from $>98\%$ to between 75\% and 86\%, reflecting the true difficulty of patient-invariant morphological generalization.

Beyond patient variation, three major clinical bottlenecks persist:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extreme Class Imbalance:} Non-arrhythmic Normal ($N$) heartbeats constitute nearly 90\% of clinical records, whereas life-critical arrhythmias such as Supraventricular Ectopic ($S$) and Fusion ($F$) beats comprise less than 3\% and 1\%, respectively. Naive loss minimization causes deep models to degenerate into majority-class classifiers.
    \item \textbf{Overconfidence and Calibration Deficit:} Modern over-parameterized neural networks tend to output highly overconfident softmax probabilities even when generating erroneous predictions \cite{b9}. In healthcare, an uncalibrated classifier is dangerous because clinical decision thresholds rely on faithful posterior probabilities.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Abstention in Ambiguous Beats:} Standard classifiers enforce a hard prediction for every single beat. In medical practice, experienced clinicians routinely seek second opinions or flag ambiguous morphologies for further diagnostic inspection. An automated model operating in isolation without uncertainty-guided referral mechanisms cannot safely integrate into clinical triage.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges, this paper presents a comprehensive, uncertainty-aware deep ensemble framework. Our principal contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Methodologically Rigorous Inter-Patient Benchmark:} We conduct all primary evaluations strictly across the de Chazal DS1/DS2 inter-patient protocol on 49,687 unseen beats, and quantify the empirical performance gap induced by data leakage against a beat-wise baseline.
    \item \textbf{Rhythm-Augmented 1D-CNN Ensemble:} We design a compact 1D-CNN architecture fusing 1.0-second normalized lead-II ECG waveforms with four domain-engineered RR-interval timing descriptors. Five independently seeded models are trained with class-frequency weighting to protect minority arrhythmic classes.
    \item \textbf{Post-Hoc Probability Calibration:} We apply validation-optimized temperature scaling to each ensemble member, demonstrating an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) reduction from 4.28\% to 3.04\%.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Quantification Benchmark \& Novel CBE Metric:} We systematically benchmark four uncertainty quantification formulations for error detection, including a novel Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) formulation that aggregates probabilities into clinical risk tiers without computational overhead.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Selective Classification:} We implement a rejection-based triage strategy, showing that referring just 10\% of the most uncertain beats to human cardiologists elevates diagnostic accuracy on retained beats from 85.86\% to 89.6\%, and up to 94.7\% under a 30\% referral budget.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section~II reviews related work. Section~III details the dataset, preprocessing, model architecture, ensemble design, calibration, and uncertainty metrics. Section~IV outlines the experimental setup. Section~V presents empirical findings and ablation analyses. Section~VI discusses clinical implications and limitations, and Section~VII concludes the study.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Classical Feature Engineering and AAMI Standards}
Early automated ECG classification systems relied heavily on manual feature extraction combined with traditional machine learning classifiers. The landmark study by de Chazal et al.\ \cite{b3} established that combining morphological features with RR-interval timing descriptors provided superior discrimination across the AAMI EC57 heartbeat superclasses \cite{b6}: Normal ($N$), Supraventricular Ectopic ($S$), Ventricular Ectopic ($V$), Fusion ($F$), and Unknown/Paced ($Q$). Subsequent investigations explored discrete wavelet transforms (DWT), higher-order statistics (HOS), and morphological filter banks coupled with Support Vector Machines (SVM) \cite{b14} and Random Forests. While interpretable, these feature engineering pipelines require delicate manual tuning and often exhibit vulnerability to baseline wander, electromyographic noise, and electrode motion artifacts.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Arrhythmia Detection}
With the proliferation of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) became widely adopted for end-to-end ECG analysis. Kiranyaz et al.\ \cite{b4} introduced patient-specific 1D-CNNs, demonstrating that dedicated convolutional filters could extract discriminating patterns directly from the raw voltage signal. Acharya et al.\ \cite{b5} proposed an 11-layer CNN achieving remarkable accuracy on the MIT-BIH database. Subsequent studies combined CNN feature extractors with Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) networks to capture temporal sequence dependencies across successive heartbeats \cite{b15}. More recently, attention mechanisms and Transformer architectures have been applied to long-duration ECG recordings. However, a major limitation across many of these studies is their reliance on beat-wise random cross-validation, which inadvertently inflates diagnostic metrics through intra-patient data leakage \cite{b12,b13}.

\subsection{Data Leakage in Biomedical Signals}
The phenomenon of intra-patient data leakage in ECG classification was systematically analyzed by Saadatpour et al.\ \cite{b12} and further corroborated by Luz et al.\ \cite{b13}. Because heartbeats generated by the same myocardial substrate share nearly identical morphological conduction delays and vector projections, testing a model on beats from the same patient that supplied training samples violates the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) assumption. When evaluated on the rigorous inter-patient split DS1/DS2, where no subject appears in both training and test partitions, reported sensitivities for minority arrhythmias (such as $S$ and $F$) typically plummet. Our work directly embraces this harder, clinically realistic problem formulation.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification and Calibration in Medical AI}
Deep learning models deployed in safety-critical medical domains must provide reliable estimates of their predictive uncertainty \cite{b7,b8}. Modern deep architectures are known to be systematically miscalibrated, often producing extreme softmax probabilities for erroneous predictions \cite{b9}. To counteract this, post-hoc temperature scaling was proposed by Guo et al.\ \cite{b9} as an effective non-parametric calibration technique. For uncertainty quantification (UQ), Gal and Ghahramani \cite{b8} introduced Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout as an approximation of Bayesian neural networks. Lakshminarayanan et al.\ \cite{b7} demonstrated that Deep Ensembles---ensembles of neural networks trained with randomized parameter initializations---consistently outperform Bayesian approximations in calibration and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. In this study, we operationalize deep ensembles for ECG classification, benchmark multiple uncertainty metrics, and introduce a risk-stratified entropy formulation tailored to cardiac diagnostics.

\subsection{Inter-Patient Benchmark Partitioning}
We evaluate our system on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database \cite{b2}, which contains 48 dual-channel ambulatory ECG recordings sampled at 360\,Hz from 47 unique subjects. Each recording has a duration of 30 minutes with beat annotations verified by independent cardiologists.

Following the standard AAMI EC57 recommendation \cite{b6} and the de Chazal partitioning protocol \cite{b3}, recordings are strictly separated into two non-overlapping cohorts of 22 records each:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DS1 (Training Cohort):} Records 101, 106, 108, 109, 112, 114, 115, 116, 118, 119, 122, 124, 201, 203, 205, 207, 208, 209, 215, 220, 223, and 230.
    \item \textbf{DS2 (Test Cohort):} Records 100, 103, 105, 111, 113, 117, 121, 123, 200, 202, 210, 212, 213, 214, 219, 221, 222, 228, 231, 232, 233, and 234.
\end{itemize}
In accordance with AAMI guidelines, four records with paced rhythms (102, 104, 107, 217) are excluded from the primary arrhythmia classes. To enable rigorous hyperparameter selection and post-hoc calibration without contaminating the test set, we partition DS1 into a training subset (18 records, 42,239 beats) and an independent patient-level validation subset (records 114, 124, 205, 223; totaling 8,756 beats). The test cohort DS2 consists of 49,687 beats from 22 subjects completely unseen during model training. Table~\ref{tab:split} presents the class distribution across partitions.

\subsection{Signal Preprocessing and Segmentation}
Raw ECG signals from the Modified Limb Lead II (MLII) channel are processed to eliminate out-of-band physiological artifacts:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bandpass Filtering:} A zero-phase 2nd-order Butterworth bandpass filter configured with cutoff frequencies at 0.5\,Hz and 45\,Hz is applied using forward-backward second-order sections (\texttt{sosfiltfilt}). This suppresses baseline wander induced by patient respiration ($<0.5$\,Hz) and high-frequency powerline interference and electromyographic noise ($>45$\,Hz).
    \item \textbf{Beat Window Segmentation:} Utilizing annotated R-peak locations $s_i$, we extract a temporal window of 360 samples ($[-180, +180]$ samples around $s_i$). At 360\,Hz sampling rate, this corresponds to a 1.0-second physiological window capturing the P-wave, QRS complex, and T-wave.
    \item \textbf{Z-Score Normalization:} To eliminate patient-specific amplitude scaling and electrode impedance variations, each extracted segment $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{360}$ is normalized to zero mean and unit variance:
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i - \mu_i}{\sigma_i + \epsilon}, \quad \epsilon = 10^{-8}.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Auxiliary Rhythm Context Features}
A prominent diagnostic failure mode in inter-patient ECG classification is the morphological resemblance between Normal ($N$) and Supraventricular Ectopic ($S$) beats. Ectopic pacemakers originating in the atria conduct through normal ventricular pathways (His-Purkinje system), generating QRS complexes morphologically identical to normal sinus beats. Consequently, pure waveform analysis cannot reliably detect $S$ beats on unseen patients.

To resolve this ambiguity, we compute four domain-engineered rhythm timing features from the R-peak sequence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-beat RR interval:} $\Delta t_{\text{pre}} = (s_i - s_{i-1}) / f_s$.
    \item \textbf{Post-beat RR interval:} $\Delta t_{\text{post}} = (s_{i+1} - s_i) / f_s$.
    \item \textbf{Local context mean RR:} Computed over a sliding window of up to 10 preceding beats:
    \begin{equation}
    \overline{\text{RR}}_{\text{local}} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \Delta t_{i-k}, \quad K = \min(i, 10).
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Prematurity ratio:} $r_{\text{pre}} = \Delta t_{\text{pre}} / \max(\overline{\text{RR}}_{\text{local}}, 10^{-3})$.
\end{itemize}
These four values form an auxiliary feature vector $\mathbf{r}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$, concatenated directly into the dense classification layer of the neural network.

\subsection{1D-CNN Backbone Architecture}
Each member of our ensemble employs a tailored 1D-CNN architecture designed to balance expressive representation with computational efficiency for real-time edge processing. The architecture contains three convolutional feature extraction blocks followed by dense classification layers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conv Block 1:} Conv1D(in=1, out=32, kernel=5, pad=2) $\to$ BatchNorm1D $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool1D(pool=2).
    \item \textbf{Conv Block 2:} Conv1D(in=32, out=64, kernel=5, pad=2) $\to$ BatchNorm1D $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool1D(pool=2).
    \item \textbf{Conv Block 3:} Conv1D(in=64, out=128, kernel=5, pad=2) $\to$ BatchNorm1D $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool1D(pool=2).
\end{itemize}
The output feature maps ($128 \times 45 = 5,760$ dimensions) are flattened and concatenated with the 4-dimensional RR-interval timing vector $\mathbf{r}_i$, producing a 5,764-dimensional combined representation. This vector is fed into two fully-connected layers:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{h}_1 &= \text{Dropout}_{0.5}\Bigl(\text{ReLU}\bigl(W_{\text{fc1}} [\mathbf{h}_{\text{conv}}; \mathbf{r}_i] + \mathbf{b}_1\bigr)\Bigr), \\
\mathbf{z} &= W_{\text{fc2}} \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2,
\end{align}
where $W_{\text{fc1}} \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 5764}$, $W_{\text{fc2}} \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 256}$, and $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^5$ denotes the unnormalized logit vector.

\subsection{Class-Weighted Objective Function}
Due to the severe class imbalance in DS1 (where class $N$ comprises 89.7\% and class $Q$ comprises 0.02\%), standard cross-entropy loss causes the network to collapse toward the majority class. We employ class-weighted cross-entropy loss, with class weights inversely proportional to training frequency:
\begin{equation}
w_c = \frac{N_{\text{total}}}{C \cdot N_c}, \quad c \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\},
\end{equation}
where $C = 5$ is the number of classes, $N_{\text{total}} = 42,239$, and $N_c$ is the frequency of class $c$ in the training set. The loss for sample $i$ with ground-truth label $y_i$ is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_i = - w_{y_i} \log \left(\frac{\exp(z_{i, y_i})}{\sum_{c=1}^C \exp(z_{i, c})}\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Deep Ensemble and Temperature Scaling Calibration}
To provide epistemic uncertainty and enhance generalization, we construct an ensemble of $M = 5$ models. Each model $m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ is trained with an independent pseudo-random weight initialization seed ($\text{seed} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$).

Although ensembling improves accuracy, individual models often produce miscalibrated probabilities. To ensure trustworthy confidence values, we apply post-hoc temperature scaling \cite{b9}. For each model $m$, a scalar parameter $T_m > 0$ is learned by minimizing the Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) on the held-out validation cohort (DS1 validation set, 8,756 beats) while keeping network weights frozen:
\begin{equation}
T_m^* = \arg\min_{T > 0} -\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{val}}} \log \left(\frac{\exp(z_{m, i, y_i} / T)}{\sum_{c=1}^C \exp(z_{m, i, c} / T)}\right).
\end{equation}
The optimal learned temperatures across our five models were found to be $T = [0.836, 1.183, 1.487, 0.979, 1.325]$. The ensemble's final predictive distribution is obtained by averaging the calibrated posterior probabilities:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \frac{\exp\bigl(z_{m, c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}) / T_m\bigr)}{\sum_{k=1}^C \exp\bigl(z_{m, k}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}) / T_m\bigr)}.
\label{eq:ensemble_prob}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Uncertainty Formulations and Cluster-Based Entropy}
We formulate and evaluate four uncertainty estimation metrics $u(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^+$:

\textbf{1) Softmax Confidence (SC):} Computed as the complement of the maximum ensemble posterior probability:
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{SC}} = 1 - \max_{c \in \{1,\dots,C\}} p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}).
\end{equation}

\textbf{2) Predictive Entropy (PE):} Measures total predictive uncertainty (aleatoric plus epistemic) across all classes:
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{PE}} = -\sum_{c=1}^C p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}) \log_2 p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}).
\end{equation}

\textbf{3) Monte Carlo Dropout Variance (MCD):} Evaluates epistemic model uncertainty by performing $T_{\text{mc}} = 20$ stochastic forward passes per ensemble member with dropout enabled ($p = 0.5$):
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{MCD}} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C \text{Var}_{t}\bigl[p_t(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r})\bigr].
\end{equation}

\textbf{4) Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) [Proposed]:} In clinical cardiology, misclassifying a Ventricular beat as Fusion carries vastly different clinical risk than misclassifying a Ventricular beat as Normal. Standard entropy treats all inter-class probability dispersion equally. We introduce Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE), which groups the five AAMI classes into four clinical risk tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tier 0 (Benign/Baseline): Normal ($\{N\}$)
    \item Tier 1 (Supraventricular Risk): ($\{S\}$)
    \item Tier 2 (Ventricular Arrhythmic Risk): Ventricular and Fusion ($\{V, F\}$)
    \item Tier 3 (Paced/Indeterminate): ($\{Q\}$)
\end{itemize}
The clustered probability distribution $\mathbf{p}_{\text{clust}} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{\text{clust}} = \Bigl[p_N,\; p_S,\; p_V + p_F,\; p_Q\Bigr]^T.
\end{equation}
CBE is then computed as the Shannon entropy of this risk-clustered distribution:
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{CBE}} = -\sum_{k=0}^3 p_{\text{clust}, k} \log_2 \bigl(p_{\text{clust}, k} + \epsilon\bigr).
\end{equation}
CBE captures clinically actionable diagnostic ambiguity across pathological categories while suppressing non-hazardous probability shifts between morphologically overlapping ventricular subclasses ($V$ and $F$). Notably, CBE requires zero additional forward passes, running at the same low latency as standard inference.

\subsection{Selective Classification and Human-in-the-Loop Referral}
In practical clinical environments, automated systems need not classify every ambiguous sample. We implement a selective classification decision rule with rejection threshold $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}(\mathbf{x}) = 
\begin{cases}
\arg\max_c p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}), & \text{if } u(\mathbf{x}) \le \tau \\
\text{\textsc{ReferToClinician}}, & \text{if } u(\mathbf{x}) > \tau
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
By tuning $\tau$ to achieve a designated referral budget $r \in [0.05, 0.30]$, a fraction $r$ of the most uncertain heartbeats are flagged for expert cardiological review, while the automated algorithm renders high-confidence verdicts on the remaining $1-r$ fraction.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Implementation Details and Hyperparameters}
The entire pipeline was implemented in Python using PyTorch and Scikit-Learn. Models were trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate $\eta = 10^{-3}$, batch size of 128, and a weight decay of $10^{-4}$. Training was executed for up to 25 epochs per member with early stopping based on validation Macro-F1 (patience of 5 epochs). All experiments were executed on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti GPU (4GB VRAM) and AMD Ryzen 7 5800H processor.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Because overall accuracy is misleading in heavily imbalanced cohorts, we evaluate both per-class and aggregate metrics on the unseen DS2 test set:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Per-Class Metrics:} Sensitivity / Recall ($\text{Sens} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP}+\text{FN}}$), Positive Predictive Value / Precision ($\text{PPV} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP}+\text{FP}}$), and F1-Score ($\text{F1} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{PPV} \cdot \text{Sens}}{\text{PPV} + \text{Sens}}$).
    \item \textbf{Macro F1:} Unweighted average of F1 scores across classes with non-zero support: $\text{Macro-F1} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C \text{F1}_c$.
    \item \textbf{Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL):} $\text{NLL} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \log p(y_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$.
    \item \textbf{Brier Score:} Mean squared error between predicted posterior probability vectors and one-hot true label vectors.
    \item \textbf{Expected Calibration Error (ECE):} Partitioning predictions into $B = 15$ confidence bins:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{ECE} = \sum_{b=1}^B \frac{|B_b|}{N} \bigl|\text{acc}(B_b) - \text{conf}(B_b)\bigr|.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Error Detection Quality:} Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUROC), Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUPRC), and Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC), evaluating how effectively uncertainty scores identify diagnostic misclassifications.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Results and Analysis}

\subsection{Diagnostic Performance on Unseen Patients (DS2)}
Table~\ref{tab:perclass} details the performance of our 5-model calibrated ensemble on the 49,687 unseen test beats of DS2. The system achieves an overall classification accuracy of \textbf{85.86\%} and a Macro-averaged F1 of \textbf{0.371}.

Normal beats ($N$) achieve a strong F1-score of 0.924 (95.6\% precision, 89.5\% recall on 44,235 beats). Ventricular ectopic beats ($V$), which carry high clinical lethality risk (such as runs of ventricular premature beats triggering ventricular tachycardia), achieve an F1-score of 0.809 with high sensitivity (87.7\% recall, 75.1\% precision on 3,220 beats).

Conversely, Supraventricular ectopic beats ($S$) achieve an F1-score of 0.098 (13.0\% recall, 7.9\% precision), and Fusion beats ($F$) achieve 0.022. As discussed in Section~III-C, $S$ beats present near-identical QRS morphology to $N$ beats, making inter-patient generalization notoriously challenging. Furthermore, class $Q$ contains only 7 test beats in DS2, resulting in zero true positives. These numbers provide an unvarnished, authentic reflection of state-of-the-art 1D-CNN capabilities under genuine inter-patient separation.

\subsection{Quantifying the Intra-Patient Data Leakage Distortion}

This empirical gap demonstrates that beat-wise evaluations largely measure a model's ability to memorize patient-specific morphological signatures rather than genuine arrhythmia pathology.

\section{Clinical Discussion and Practical Implications}

\subsection{Translational Viability in Ambulatory Holter Triage}
In ambulatory telemetry services, cardiologists review records containing 100,000+ beats per patient-day. A fully autonomous system with an error rate of $\sim$14\% is unacceptable for unmonitored diagnosis. However, an automated triaging agent operating at a 10\% referral threshold successfully resolves 90\% of beats with $\sim$90\% accuracy, while flagging the 10\% most challenging segments for human review. This achieves a 10-fold reduction in clinical interpretation workload while mitigating physician diagnostic fatigue.

\subsection{Pathological Underpinnings of Minority Class Errors}
Our per-class findings shed light on biological mechanisms underlying residual classification errors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supraventricular Beats ($S$):} Ectopic foci originate above the bifurcation of the bundle of His. Ventricular depolarization occurs via normal pathways, creating QRS signatures indistinguishable from sinus rhythm. Although RR-interval features partially alleviate this ambiguity, heart rate variability, respiratory sinus arrhythmia, and conduction blocks obscure timing clues across unseen patients.
    \item \textbf{Fusion Beats ($F$):} Fusion occurs when normal sinus conduction and ectopic ventricular pacemaker impulses coincide within the ventricles, producing hybrid waveforms. Because $F$ morphology spans a continuous continuum between $N$ and $V$, distinct decision boundaries are difficult to establish.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational Efficiency for Embedded Healthcare Devices}
Our 5-member 1D-CNN ensemble contains only $\sim$1.5 million parameters per member (checkpoint size $\sim$5.8\,MB). Batch inference on 49,687 heartbeats executes in less than 4 seconds on a standard desktop GPU and under 30 seconds on an embedded CPU. Furthermore, our proposed Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) metric computes uncertainty directly from existing ensemble logits without additional stochastic passes (unlike MC Dropout, which requires 20 forward passes). This makes our framework well-suited for deployment on battery-powered wearable ECG patches and Holter monitors.

\subsection{Limitations}
This investigation has several limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-Lead Restriction:} Analysis was restricted to Modified Limb Lead II (MLII). Multi-lead ECG acquisitions (e.g., standard 12-lead or 3-lead Holter setups) capture spatial vector loops that improve distinction between $S$ and $N$ morphologies.
    \item \textbf{Cohort Homogeneity:} While DS1/DS2 provides rigorous inter-patient separation, all records originate from the MIT-BIH dataset. Cross-database validation on independent repositories (e.g., INCART, PTB-XL, and Chapman-Shaoxing) is required to evaluate resilience against diverse acquisition hardware.
    \item \textbf{Under-represented $Q$ Class:} Class $Q$ contained only 7 test samples in DS2, precluding statistically robust conclusions for this specific category.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This paper has presented a calibrated deep ensemble framework for ECG arrhythmia detection, evaluated under the rigorous inter-patient DS1/DS2 protocol on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. On 49,687 heartbeats from 22 completely unseen subjects, the system achieves an overall accuracy of 85.86\% and a Macro F1-score of 0.371, outperforming unweighted and single-model baselines. We demonstrated that beat-wise random splitting produces a 12.5 percentage-point artificial inflation in accuracy, cautioning researchers against contaminated evaluation practices.

Post-hoc temperature scaling reduced the Expected Calibration Error from 4.28\% to 3.04\%, delivering calibrated posterior probabilities suitable for clinical decision support. Benchmarking four uncertainty quantification formulations demonstrated that Softmax Confidence achieves an error-detection AUROC of 0.819, while the proposed Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) matches predictive entropy performance without extra computational overhead. Integrating uncertainty-based selective classification confirmed that referring the 10\% most uncertain beats to clinical cardiologists elevates diagnostic accuracy on retained beats to 89.6\%, and up to 94.7\% at 30\% referral. Future work will investigate multi-lead spatial fusion and cross-database validation on large-scale international ECG repositories.
