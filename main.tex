% ABSTRACT
% ==================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Automated electrocardiogram (ECG) arrhythmia classification could support ambulatory Holter monitoring, but many studies report inflated results because random beat-wise splits leak patient-specific morphology into the test set. This paper builds an uncertainty-aware ensemble of five 1D convolutional neural networks (1D-CNNs). Each network takes a bandpass-filtered lead-II ECG window together with four RR-interval timing features and is trained with class-weighted cross-entropy. The system is evaluated on the inter-patient DS1/DS2 protocol of de Chazal et al.\ on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. On 49,687 test beats, the ensemble reaches 85.86\% accuracy and a macro F1 of 0.371 (0.463 over the four classes with adequate support). Minority classes remain poorly detected, with F1 of 0.098 for supraventricular and 0.022 for fusion beats. An earlier beat-wise evaluation on a different pipeline reported 98.4\% accuracy, which shows how much random splitting can overstate performance. Per-member temperature scaling gives a modest reduction in Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 4.28\% to 3.04\%, although the Brier score does not improve. We compare four uncertainty scores for error detection, including a cluster-based entropy (CBE) that groups the five classes into four clinical tiers. Softmax confidence is the best detector (AUROC 0.819) and CBE is slightly lower (0.798). Referring the 10\% most uncertain beats raises accuracy on the retained beats from 85.86\% to 89.6\%, and a 30\% referral rate gives 94.7\%, but macro F1 rises only to about 0.42.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ECG arrhythmia classification, deep ensemble, uncertainty quantification, temperature scaling, selective classification, inter-patient evaluation, MIT-BIH, AAMI EC57.

\section{Introduction}

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of global mortality, responsible for approximately 17.9 million deaths annually \cite{b1}. Cardiac arrhythmias, which are disturbances in the electrical rhythm of the heart, frequently precede acute events such as cardiac arrest and ischemic stroke. Ambulatory electrocardiography (for example 24- to 48-hour Holter monitoring) is standard practice for capturing intermittent arrhythmias. A typical 24-hour recording contains over 100,000 heartbeats, so manual inspection by cardiologists is labor-intensive, costly, and prone to fatigue.

Deep learning, and in particular 1D convolutional neural networks (1D-CNNs), has shown strong potential for heartbeat classification directly from raw recordings \cite{b4,b5}. A methodological issue limits the clinical meaning of many reported results, namely \emph{intra-patient data leakage} \cite{b3,b12,b13}. When heartbeats are shuffled randomly between training and test sets, beats from the same patient appear on both sides. Because each patient has an idiosyncratic conduction morphology, the network can memorize patient identity instead of learning arrhythmia patterns, and performance falls sharply on unseen patients.

To avoid this, de Chazal et al.\ \cite{b3} proposed the inter-patient DS1/DS2 protocol, in line with AAMI EC57 guidelines \cite{b6}. The 44 recordings are divided into two disjoint groups of 22 records, one for training (DS1) and one for testing (DS2).

Three further problems matter for clinical use.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Class imbalance.} Normal ($N$) beats make up about 90\% of the 44 evaluated records, whereas supraventricular ($S$) and fusion ($F$) beats make up about 2.8\% and 0.8\%. In our training set the ratio of $N$ to $S$ beats is about 46:1 and of $N$ to $F$ beats about 100:1.
    \item \textbf{Overconfidence.} Modern networks produce softmax scores that are often overconfident \cite{b9}.
    \item \textbf{No abstention.} A system that must label every beat cannot defer ambiguous ones to a clinician.
\end{enumerate}

This paper studies these problems together under the strict inter-patient protocol. Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We report performance of a five-member 1D-CNN ensemble on 49,687 beats from unseen DS2 records, including the weak results on minority classes, and we contrast it with an earlier beat-wise result.
    \item We combine the raw ECG window with four RR-interval features and train with class-weighted loss.
    \item We apply per-member temperature scaling and analyze calibration with ECE, NLL, and Brier score.
    \item We benchmark four uncertainty scores for error detection, including a cluster-based entropy (CBE) that merges classes into four clinical tiers. In our experiments softmax confidence is the best detector and CBE is close but not better.
    \item We analyze selective classification and show that referral improves accuracy substantially but macro F1 only slightly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{ECG Classification and AAMI Standards}
Early automated methods relied on hand-crafted morphological and interval features with neural-network classifiers \cite{b14} or linear discriminants \cite{b3}. The AAMI EC57 standard \cite{b6} groups heartbeats into five superclasses, namely Normal ($N$), Supraventricular ectopic ($S$), Ventricular ectopic ($V$), Fusion ($F$), and Unknown/Paced ($Q$). Deep models later removed manual feature design. Kiranyaz et al.\ \cite{b4} used patient-specific 1D-CNNs, Acharya et al.\ \cite{b5} proposed a deep CNN, and Yildirim et al.\ \cite{b15} combined CNNs with LSTM networks.

\subsection{Data Leakage and Inter-Patient Evaluation}
Random beat-wise splits give optimistic results that do not transfer to unseen patients \cite{b13}. Mousavi and Afghah compared inter-patient and intra-patient evaluation for deep models \cite{b12}. Under the DS1/DS2 protocol \cite{b3}, the absence of patient overlap exposes the difficulty of morphological variability between patients, especially for minority classes.

\subsection{Uncertainty, Calibration, and Selective Classification}
Calibrated confidence is important in medicine \cite{b7,b8,b9}. Deep ensembles \cite{b7} give strong uncertainty estimates and were found to be more robust than MC Dropout \cite{b8} in their experiments. Temperature scaling \cite{b9} rescales logits with a single scalar and does not change the predicted class. Selective classification lets a model abstain on uncertain inputs and is evaluated with risk--coverage curves \cite{b17}. We apply these ideas to inter-patient ECG classification.

\subsection{Dataset and Partitioning Protocol}
We use the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database \cite{b2,b11}, which has 48 half-hour two-channel recordings from 47 subjects sampled at 360\,Hz. Following AAMI EC57 \cite{b6} and de Chazal et al.\ \cite{b3}, the records are split as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DS1 (training cohort):} records 101, 106, 108, 109, 112, 114, 115, 116, 118, 119, 122, 124, 201, 203, 205, 207, 208, 209, 215, 220, 223, and 230.
    \item \textbf{DS2 (test cohort):} records 100, 103, 105, 111, 113, 117, 121, 123, 200, 202, 210, 212, 213, 214, 219, 221, 222, 228, 231, 232, 233, and 234.
\end{itemize}
The four paced records (102, 104, 107, 217) are excluded. DS1 is divided into a training set (18 records, 42,239 beats) and a patient-level validation set (records 114, 124, 205, and 223, with 8,756 beats). The validation set is used for early stopping and temperature scaling. The test set DS2 contains 49,687 beats (Table~\ref{tab:split}). Records 201 (DS1) and 202 (DS2) come from the same subject, so the test cohort contains 21 fully unseen subjects and one subject whose other record is in training.

\subsection{Preprocessing and RR-Interval Features}
The Modified Limb Lead II (MLII) signal is filtered with a zero-phase 2nd-order Butterworth bandpass filter (0.5--45\,Hz). A 360-sample window ($\pm180$ samples, 1.0\,s) is cut around each annotated R-peak and normalized to zero mean and unit variance. R-peak positions come from the reference annotations, so the results assume correct beat detection. A deployed system would need an automatic detector such as Pan--Tompkins \cite{b10}.

Supraventricular beats have QRS morphology close to normal beats, so timing information is useful. We compute four features from the R-peak series $s_i$ (in samples):
\begin{align}
\Delta t_{\text{pre}} &= (s_i - s_{i-1}) / 360, \\
\Delta t_{\text{post}} &= (s_{i+1} - s_i) / 360, \\
\overline{\text{RR}}_{\text{local}} &= \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \Delta t_{i-k}, \quad K = \min(i, 10), \\
r_{\text{pre}} &= \Delta t_{\text{pre}} / \max(\overline{\text{RR}}_{\text{local}}, 10^{-3}).
\end{align}
Together they form a vector $\mathbf{r}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ that is concatenated to the flattened convolutional features.

\subsection{1D-CNN Backbone and Class-Weighted Loss}
Each ensemble member has three blocks, each consisting of Conv1D (kernel 5, padding 2), batch normalization, ReLU, and max pooling (2), with 32, 64, and 128 filters. The flattened feature map ($128 \times 45 = 5{,}760$) is concatenated with the four RR features and passed through Dense(256), ReLU, Dropout(0.5), and Dense(5) to give logits $\mathbf{z}$. Each model has about 1.5 million parameters. Training uses class-weighted cross-entropy with $w_c = N_{\text{total}} / (C \cdot N_c)$. Because class $Q$ has only 8 training beats, its weight is very large.

\subsection{Calibrated Ensemble}
Five models ($M=5$) are trained with seeds 0 to 4 using Adam \cite{b16} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 128) and early stopping with patience 5 on validation macro F1. For each member, a temperature $T_m > 0$ is fitted on the DS1 validation set by minimizing negative log-likelihood (NLL) \cite{b9}. The fitted temperatures are $T = [0.836, 1.183, 1.487, 0.979, 1.325]$. The ensemble posterior averages the temperature-scaled logits:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}) = \mathrm{softmax}_c\!\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \frac{z_{m}}{T_m}\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Uncertainty Scores}
Four scores $u(\mathbf{x})$ are compared, where a higher value means a more uncertain beat.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Softmax confidence (SC):} $u_{\text{SC}} = 1 - \max_c p_{\text{ens}}(c \mid \mathbf{x})$.
    \item \textbf{Predictive entropy (PE):} $u_{\text{PE}} = -\sum_c p_{\text{ens}}(c \mid \mathbf{x}) \log_2 p_{\text{ens}}(c \mid \mathbf{x})$.
    \item \textbf{MC Dropout variance (MCD):} variance across 20 stochastic forward passes with dropout ($p=0.5$).
    \item \textbf{Cluster-based entropy (CBE):} the class probabilities are merged into four clinical tiers, $\{N\}$, $\{S\}$, $\{V,F\}$, and $\{Q\}$, before computing entropy:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{\text{clust}} = [p_N,\; p_S,\; p_V + p_F,\; p_Q]^T,
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
    u_{\text{CBE}} = -\sum_{k=0}^3 p_{\text{clust}, k} \log_2 (p_{\text{clust}, k} + \epsilon).
    \end{equation}
    CBE ignores uncertainty between $V$ and $F$ and reacts to ambiguity between tiers. Like SC and PE it needs no extra forward passes. To our knowledge, grouped entropy of this kind has not been studied for ECG beat classification.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Selective Classification and Metrics}
For a referral budget $r \in [0.05, 0.30]$, the $r$-fraction of test beats with the highest uncertainty is referred to a clinician and the model predicts on the remaining $1-r$. We report the following.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{AUROC and AUPRC} for detecting misclassified beats (errors are the positive class).
    \item \textbf{AURC,} the area under the risk--coverage curve (lower is better) \cite{b17}.
    \item \textbf{Acc@$r$,} the accuracy on the retained beats at referral rate $r$.
\end{itemize}
ECE uses equal-width confidence bins (15 bins). In deployment, the threshold on $u$ would be set on validation data. Here the referral budget is applied directly to the test ranking.

\subsection{Performance on Unseen Patients}
Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cm} give the results on 49,687 DS2 beats. The ensemble reaches 85.86\% accuracy and a macro F1 of 0.371. Normal beats reach F1 of 0.924 and ventricular beats reach 0.809 with 0.877 recall. Minority classes are poorly detected, with F1 of 0.098 for $S$ and 0.022 for $F$. Most $S$ and $F$ beats are labeled as normal or ventricular. Class $Q$ has only 7 test beats and is never detected, which lowers the five-class macro F1. Over the four classes with adequate support the macro F1 is 0.463.

An earlier version of this system, evaluated with a beat-wise random split on a different preprocessing pipeline, reported 98.4\% accuracy. The gap of 12.5 points is consistent with the leakage effect described in the literature \cite{b3,b13}. Because the two pipelines differ in preprocessing, included records, and features, this gap is indicative and is not a controlled measurement of leakage alone.

\subsection{Ensemble Effect and Calibration}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_calib} compares a single model with the ensemble. Both use RR features and class weighting. Averaging five members raises accuracy from 79.3\% to 85.9\% (6.6 points) and macro F1 from 0.329 to 0.371, while $S$-class F1 changes little (0.080 to 0.098). The single-model row uses one seed, so seed variance is not captured. The separate effects of RR features and class weighting were not isolated and are left for future work.

Temperature scaling gives a modest improvement in calibration. ECE falls from 4.28\% to 3.04\% (a 29\% relative reduction) and NLL from 0.4113 to 0.4020. The Brier score rises slightly from 0.2071 to 0.2091. Two of the five fitted temperatures are below 1 (0.836 and 0.979), so not all members were overconfident on the validation records. The temperatures were fitted on only four validation records and applied to a different test cohort, which may limit the gain.

\subsection{Uncertainty Scores and Selective Classification}
Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} and Fig.~\ref{fig:referral} summarize error detection and referral. Softmax confidence is the best error detector on every metric (AUROC 0.819, AUPRC 0.405). Predictive entropy follows with AUROC 0.810. CBE reaches AUROC 0.798 and equals predictive entropy at 10\% referral (88.8\%), but it is slightly lower at 30\% referral (94.0\% against 94.7\%). MC Dropout variance is the weakest (AUROC 0.754). CBE therefore does not improve on softmax confidence in our experiments. Its possible value is that it is tied to clinical risk tiers, and a targeted analysis of which confusions it catches is left for future work.

Referring the 10\% most uncertain beats raises accuracy on the retained beats from 85.86\% to 89.6\% (macro F1 about 0.39), and a 30\% referral rate gives 94.7\% (macro F1 about 0.42). The accuracy gain is mainly from removing errors on the dominant normal and ventricular classes. Macro F1 changes only slightly, which shows that the minority classes $S$ and $F$ are still missed after referral. Uncertainty-based referral therefore reduces the burden of review but does not fix the weak detection of minority classes.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Weak minority classes.} Recall for $S$ and $F$ is 0.130 and 0.054. Better handling of these classes is needed before any clinical use.
    \item \textbf{Single run.} Results come from one ensemble, without confidence intervals or tests of significance.
    \item \textbf{Ablation scope.} The effects of RR features, class weighting, and the number of members were not studied separately.
    \item \textbf{Single database and lead.} Only MIT-BIH and the MLII lead are used, and no cross-database validation (for example INCART or PTB-XL) is reported. We do not compare against other published inter-patient methods.
    \item \textbf{Annotated R-peaks.} Beat positions come from reference annotations.
    \item \textbf{Class $Q$ and subject overlap.} Class $Q$ has 7 test beats, and records 201 and 202 share a subject.
    \item \textbf{Not clinically validated.} The system is a research prototype evaluated on a public database only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented a calibrated deep ensemble of 1D-CNNs with RR-interval features for ECG arrhythmia classification, evaluated on the inter-patient DS1/DS2 protocol. On 49,687 beats from unseen records, it achieves 85.86\% accuracy and 0.371 macro F1, with weak detection of supraventricular and fusion beats. Temperature scaling gives a modest ECE reduction, and softmax confidence is the best of four uncertainty scores for detecting errors, with CBE close behind. Referring the most uncertain beats to a clinician raises accuracy on retained beats to 89.6\% at 10\% referral and 94.7\% at 30\%, but macro F1 improves little. Future work includes isolating the effect of each design choice, improving minority-class detection, reporting results over several seeds with confidence intervals, and validating on other databases.
