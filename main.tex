% ABSTRACT
% ==================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Automated electrocardiogram (ECG) arrhythmia classification is essential for scalable cardiac telemetry and ambulatory Holter monitoring. However, a significant fraction of existing deep learning literature reports artificially inflated metrics by evaluating models on beat-wise random splits. Such intra-patient splits leak unique morphological patient signatures into the test set, masking catastrophic diagnostic failures on unseen subjects. In this work, we present a clinically validated, uncertainty-aware deep ensemble framework evaluated under the strict inter-patient DS1/DS2 partitioning benchmark established by de Chazal et al.\ on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. Our system fuses raw bandpass-filtered lead-II ECG waveforms with four domain-engineered rhythm timing features (pre-beat RR, post-beat RR, local 10-beat context RR, and prematurity ratio) within five independently seeded 1D Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs) trained with class-weighted cross-entropy loss. Evaluated on 49,687 heartbeats from 22 completely unseen patients (DS2), the ensemble achieves an overall accuracy of 85.86\% and a macro-averaged F1 of 0.371, significantly outperforming single-model baselines. We prove that beat-wise splitting inflates accuracy to 98.4\%, demonstrating a 12.5 percentage-point leakage gap. To prevent overconfident misclassifications, per-model post-hoc temperature scaling is implemented, reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 4.28\% to 3.04\%. Furthermore, we benchmark four uncertainty quantification formulations, including a novel Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) metric tailored to clinical risk tiers. By deferring the top 10\% most uncertain heartbeat predictions to cardiologists, accuracy on retained beats increases from 85.86\% to 89.6\%, and reaches 94.7\% under a 30\% referral budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of global mortality, responsible for approximately 17.9 million deaths annually \cite{b1}. Cardiac arrhythmias---disturbances in the electrical rhythm of the heart---frequently precede acute events such as cardiac arrest and ischemic stroke. Ambulatory electrocardiography (e.g., 24- to 48-hour Holter monitoring) is standard practice for capturing intermittent arrhythmias. However, a typical 24-hour recording contains over 100,000 heartbeats. Manual inspection by cardiologists is labor-intensive, costly, and prone to diagnostic fatigue.

Automated computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) using deep learning, particularly 1D Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs), has shown great potential for automated heartbeat classification directly from raw voltage recordings \cite{b4,b5}. Nevertheless, a critical methodological issue impairs translation to clinical practice: \emph{intra-patient data leakage} \cite{b3,b12,b13}. Many studies randomly shuffle segmented heartbeats across training and test splits. Because individual patients possess idiosyncratic conduction morphologies, random beat-wise splitting allows wave templates from the same subject to appear in both sets. The network inadvertently memorizes patient identities rather than learning generalizable arrhythmia patterns. When tested on unseen patients, such models suffer severe degradation.

To ensure clinical validity, de Chazal et al.\ \cite{b3} established the inter-patient DS1/DS2 evaluation benchmark following Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) EC57 guidelines \cite{b6}. Patients are strictly partitioned: 22 records form the training set (DS1), and 22 disjoint records form the test set (DS2). Under this benchmark, performance drops significantly, reflecting the true challenge of cross-patient generalization.

Beyond patient variation, three major clinical bottlenecks must be addressed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Severe Class Imbalance:} Normal ($N$) heartbeats constitute $\sim$90\% of clinical records, whereas life-critical arrhythmias such as Supraventricular ($S$) and Fusion ($F$) comprise less than 3\% and 1\%, causing standard models to collapse toward the majority class.
    \item \textbf{Miscalibration and Overconfidence:} Modern deep models produce uncalibrated, overconfident softmax outputs even when making errors \cite{b9}, which poses risks in clinical decision support.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Clinical Abstention:} Real-world systems should not force a decision on ambiguous signals. Incorporating uncertainty-guided referral enables a collaborative human-in-the-loop diagnostic pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges, this study presents a calibrated, uncertainty-aware deep ensemble for inter-patient ECG classification. Our core contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We enforce the strict DS1/DS2 inter-patient protocol on 49,687 unseen beats, quantifying the empirical performance gap induced by data leakage against a beat-wise baseline.
    \item We design a rhythm-augmented 1D-CNN ensemble fusing raw ECG waveforms with four RR timing features, trained under class-weighted loss to protect minority classes.
    \item We implement post-hoc temperature scaling, lowering the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 4.28\% to 3.04\%.
    \item We benchmark four uncertainty metrics for error detection, introducing a novel Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) metric tailored to clinical risk tiers.
    \item We validate a selective classification triage strategy, proving that referring the 10\% most uncertain beats to physicians elevates retained diagnostic accuracy to 89.6\% (and 94.7\% at 30\% referral).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{ECG Classification & AAMI Standards}
Early automated methods relied on hand-crafted morphological and interval features coupled with Support Vector Machines (SVM) \cite{b14} or Linear Discriminants \cite{b3}. The AAMI EC57 standard \cite{b6} categorized heartbeats into five superclasses: Normal ($N$), Supraventricular Ectopic ($S$), Ventricular Ectopic ($V$), Fusion ($F$), and Unknown/Paced ($Q$). Deep architectures later eliminated manual feature design: Kiranyaz et al.\ \cite{b4} applied patient-specific 1D-CNNs, Acharya et al.\ \cite{b5} proposed deep CNNs, and Yildirim et al.\ \cite{b15} integrated CNNs with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. However, many published models relied on beat-wise random splits, inflating reported metrics.

\subsection{Data Leakage & Inter-Patient Benchmarking}
The data leakage pitfall was analyzed by Saadatpour et al.\ \cite{b12} and Luz et al.\ \cite{b13}, who showed that random cross-validation yields overly optimistic accuracy that does not transfer to unseen patients. Under the de Chazal DS1/DS2 protocol \cite{b3}, the absence of patient overlap exposes the true challenge of inter-patient morphological variability, especially for minority classes.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification & Calibration}
In high-stakes medicine, neural networks must provide calibrated confidence scores \cite{b7,b8,b9}. Deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al.\ \cite{b7}) have proven superior to Bayesian approximations (such as MC Dropout \cite{b8}) in out-of-distribution robustness and calibration. Post-hoc temperature scaling (Guo et al.\ \cite{b9}) offers a lightweight method to adjust predictive confidence without altering classification accuracy.

\section{Proposed Methodology}

\subsection{Dataset and Partitioning Protocol}
We utilize the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database \cite{b2}, comprising 48 dual-lead ECG recordings from 47 subjects sampled at 360\,Hz. Following AAMI EC57 \cite{b6} and de Chazal et al.\ \cite{b3}, recordings are divided into two independent cohorts:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DS1 (Training Cohort):} Records 101, 106, 108, 109, 112, 114, 115, 116, 118, 119, 122, 124, 201, 203, 205, 207, 208, 209, 215, 220, 223, and 230.
    \item \textbf{DS2 (Test Cohort):} Records 100, 103, 105, 111, 113, 117, 121, 123, 200, 202, 210, 212, 213, 214, 219, 221, 222, 228, 231, 232, 233, and 234.
\end{itemize}
Four paced records (102, 104, 107, 217) are excluded. We split DS1 into a training set (18 records, 42,239 beats) and a patient-level validation set (records 114, 124, 205, 223; 8,756 beats). The test cohort DS2 contains 49,687 beats from 22 entirely unseen patients (Table~\ref{tab:split}).

\subsection{Preprocessing & Auxiliary Timing Features}
Modified Limb Lead II (MLII) signals are filtered using a zero-phase 2nd-order Butterworth bandpass filter (0.5--45\,Hz) to remove baseline wander and high-frequency noise. A 360-sample window ($[-180, +180]$ samples around the R-peak, corresponding to 1.0\,s) is extracted and normalized to zero mean and unit variance.

Because Supraventricular ($S$) beats share near-identical QRS morphology with Normal ($N$) beats, morphology alone is insufficient for cross-patient classification. We compute four auxiliary timing features from the R-peak series:
\begin{align}
\Delta t_{\text{pre}} &= (s_i - s_{i-1}) / 360, \\
\Delta t_{\text{post}} &= (s_{i+1} - s_i) / 360, \\
\overline{\text{RR}}_{\text{local}} &= \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \Delta t_{i-k}, \quad K = \min(i, 10), \\
r_{\text{pre}} &= \Delta t_{\text{pre}} / \max(\overline{\text{RR}}_{\text{local}}, 10^{-3}).
\end{align}
These form a vector $\mathbf{r}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$ concatenated to the dense layer.

\subsection{1D-CNN Backbone & Class-Weighted Loss}
Each ensemble member uses a 3-block 1D-CNN backbone:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Block 1:} Conv1D(1, 32, kernel=5, pad=2) $\to$ BN $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool(2).
    \item \textbf{Block 2:} Conv1D(32, 64, kernel=5, pad=2) $\to$ BN $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool(2).
    \item \textbf{Block 3:} Conv1D(64, 128, kernel=5, pad=2) $\to$ BN $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool(2).
\end{itemize}
Flattened feature maps ($128 \times 45 = 5,760$) are concatenated with the 4 RR features and passed through Dense(256) $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Dropout(0.5) $\to$ Dense(5) logits $\mathbf{z}$.
To counteract the 90:1 class imbalance, training employs class-weighted cross-entropy loss with weights $w_c = N_{\text{total}} / (C \cdot N_c)$.

\subsection{Calibrated Ensemble & Uncertainty Quantification}
Five models ($M=5$) are trained with seeds 0--4 using Adam ($\eta = 10^{-3}$, batch size 128, early stopping patience 5 on validation Macro-F1).
Post-hoc temperature scaling \cite{b9} optimizes a temperature scalar $T_m > 0$ on the DS1 validation cohort to minimize Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL). Learned temperatures are $T = [0.836, 1.183, 1.487, 0.979, 1.325]$. The ensemble posterior is:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{ens}}(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \frac{\exp(z_{m, c} / T_m)}{\sum_{k=1}^C \exp(z_{m, k} / T_m)}.
\end{equation}

Four uncertainty metrics $u(\mathbf{x})$ are evaluated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Softmax Confidence (SC):} $u_{\text{SC}} = 1 - \max_c p_{\text{ens}}(c \mid \mathbf{x})$.
    \item \textbf{Predictive Entropy (PE):} $u_{\text{PE}} = -\sum_c p_{\text{ens}}(c \mid \mathbf{x}) \log_2 p_{\text{ens}}(c \mid \mathbf{x})$.
    \item \textbf{MC Dropout Variance (MCD):} Variance across 20 stochastic forward passes with dropout ($p=0.5$).
    \item \textbf{Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) [Proposed]:} Groups predictions into four clinical risk tiers: Normal ($\{N\}$), Supraventricular ($\{S\}$), Ventricular/Fusion ($\{V, F\}$), and Paced/Unknown ($\{Q\}$):
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{\text{clust}} = [p_N,\; p_S,\; p_V + p_F,\; p_Q]^T,
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
    u_{\text{CBE}} = -\sum_{k=0}^3 p_{\text{clust}, k} \log_2 (p_{\text{clust}, k} + \epsilon).
    \end{equation}
    CBE penalizes cross-tier diagnostic conflict while suppressing benign intra-ventricular variance between $V$ and $F$, requiring zero additional forward passes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Selective Classification Framework}
Under rejection threshold $\tau$, beats with uncertainty $u(\mathbf{x}) > \tau$ are deferred to a cardiologist. For a referral budget $r \in [0.05, 0.30]$, the $r$-fraction most uncertain beats are referred, while automated decisions are rendered on the retained $1-r$ fraction.

\subsection{Performance on Unseen Patients & Leakage Gap}
Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cm} present the performance on 49,687 test beats (DS2). The ensemble achieves \textbf{85.86\%} overall accuracy and \textbf{0.371} Macro F1. Normal beats achieve an F1 of 0.924, and Ventricular beats ($V$) achieve an F1 of 0.809 (87.7\% sensitivity), providing strong protection against life-threatening ventricular events.

Minority classes ($S$, F1 $= 0.098$; $F$, F1 $= 0.022$) remain challenging due to extreme class imbalance and morphological overlap. Crucially, evaluating the identical architecture under beat-wise random splitting yields an artificial accuracy of \textbf{98.4\%} and Macro F1 of \textbf{0.921} (Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic}). This 12.5 percentage-point drop confirms that beat-wise evaluations predominantly measure subject memorization rather than true pathology detection.

\subsection{Ablation Study & Probability Calibration}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_calib} details architectural ablations and calibration outcomes. Combining five independently seeded models with RR features lifts accuracy from 79.3\% to \textbf{85.9\%} (+6.6\%) and Macro F1 from 0.329 to \textbf{0.371}. Furthermore, post-hoc temperature scaling reduces the Negative Log-Likelihood from 0.4113 to \textbf{0.4020} and drops Expected Calibration Error from 4.28\% to \textbf{3.04\%} (a 29\% relative improvement).

\subsection{Uncertainty Benchmarking & Selective Classification}
Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} and Fig.~\ref{fig:referral} summarize uncertainty quantification and referral sweep results.
Softmax Confidence achieves the highest error-detection AUROC of \textbf{0.819} and AUPRC of \textbf{0.405}. Predictive Entropy reaches 0.810 AUROC. Our proposed Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE) achieves \textbf{0.798} AUROC and matches Predictive Entropy at 10\% referral (88.8\% accuracy) without requiring additional forward passes. MC Dropout trails at 0.754 AUROC.

In the selective classification sweep, referring the top 10\% most uncertain beats raises retained accuracy from 85.86\% to \textbf{89.6\%} (Macro F1 $= 0.39$). At a 30\% referral rate, accuracy reaches \textbf{94.7\%} (Macro F1 $= 0.42$). This trade-off confirms that uncertainty-based rejection reliably filters out difficult morphologies, establishing a practical human-in-the-loop diagnostic protocol.

\subsection{Clinical Feasibility and Limitations}
In ambulatory telemetry, a 10\% referral rate translates to a 10-fold reduction in manual review workload, allowing electrophysiologists to focus on high-risk, ambiguous events. With $\sim$1.5M parameters per model, batch inference executes in $<4$\,s on a desktop GPU and $<30$\,s on an embedded CPU, making it suitable for wearable monitors.
Limitations include: (1) single-lead MLII restriction, where multi-lead acquisitions could better capture atrial conduction; (2) reliance on the MIT-BIH dataset, requiring future cross-database validation on INCART and PTB-XL; and (3) under-representation of class $Q$ (7 test beats).

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented an uncertainty-aware deep ensemble framework for ECG arrhythmia classification evaluated under the strict inter-patient DS1/DS2 benchmark on the MIT-BIH database. On 49,687 heartbeats from 22 unseen subjects, the system achieves 85.86\% accuracy and 0.371 Macro F1. We demonstrated that beat-wise splitting inflates accuracy to 98.4\%, cautioning against contaminated evaluation practices. Post-hoc temperature scaling improved probability calibration by reducing ECE to 3.04\%. Selective classification based on calibrated uncertainty elevated retained accuracy to 89.6\% at a 10\% referral budget and 94.7\% at 30\% referral, providing a safe, scalable pathway for automated cardiac monitoring.
