\begin{abstract}
Automated electrocardiogram (ECG) arrhythmia classification is essential
for scalable cardiac monitoring, yet most published systems report inflated
metrics by using beat-wise random splits that leak patient-specific
morphology into the test set.  We present a calibrated deep ensemble of
five 1D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) evaluated under the strict
inter-patient DS1/DS2 partitioning protocol on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia
Database.  Each member incorporates class-weighted cross-entropy loss and
auxiliary RR-interval features to mitigate severe class imbalance.  On
49,687 unseen test beats the ensemble achieves an overall accuracy of
85.86\% and a macro-averaged F1 of 0.371.  Post-hoc temperature scaling
reduces the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 4.28\% to 3.04\%.
Four uncertainty metrics are benchmarked for selective classification;
softmax confidence achieves the highest error-detection AUROC of 0.819.
Referring the 10\% most uncertain beats to a clinician raises retained
accuracy to 89.6\%.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
ECG arrhythmia classification, deep ensemble, uncertainty quantification,
temperature scaling, selective classification, inter-patient evaluation,

\section{Introduction}

Cardiac arrhythmias are a leading cause of sudden cardiac death worldwide.
Automated classification of arrhythmias from ambulatory ECG recordings can
assist clinicians by triaging large volumes of Holter data.  The MIT-BIH
Arrhythmia Database \cite{b1} remains the de-facto benchmark, and the
AAMI EC57 standard \cite{b2} groups heartbeat labels into five
superclasses: Normal (N), Supraventricular ectopic (S), Ventricular
ectopic (V), Fusion (F), and Paced/Unknown (Q).

A critical but frequently overlooked issue is \emph{data leakage}.
Beat-wise random splitting allows morphologically similar beats from the
same patient to appear in both training and test sets, inflating reported
metrics.  The inter-patient DS1/DS2 protocol proposed by de~Chazal
et~al.\ \cite{b3} ensures that no patient appears in both partitions,
providing a realistic clinical performance estimate.

Despite this well-documented pitfall, many recent deep-learning surveys
still report beat-wise accuracy exceeding 98\%, which does not transfer to
unseen patients.  In this work we deliberately adopt the harder
inter-patient split and report all metrics on an entirely unseen test
cohort.

Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{calibrated 5-member deep ensemble} with class-weighted
        loss and RR-interval augmentation, evaluated under strict
        inter-patient DS1/DS2 splits.
  \item A \textbf{comprehensive uncertainty benchmark} comparing four
        uncertainty formulations on selective-classification and
        error-detection tasks.
  \item A novel \textbf{Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE)} formulation that
        requires no additional forward passes yet performs comparably to
        predictive entropy.
\end{itemize}

% ==================================================================
\section{Related Work}

Kiranyaz et~al.\ \cite{b4} demonstrated that patient-specific 1D-CNNs
achieve high accuracy, but the model must be re-trained for each new
patient.  Acharya et~al.\ \cite{b5} applied deep CNNs to the MIT-BIH
database but used beat-wise splits, yielding accuracy above 98\% that
does not generalise.  de~Chazal et~al.\ \cite{b3} established the
inter-patient protocol using hand-crafted morphological and interval
features; our work extends this to a deep ensemble with modern uncertainty
estimation.  Lakshminarayanan et~al.\ \cite{b6} introduced deep ensembles
as a simple baseline for uncertainty quantification; we adapt their
framework to the class-imbalanced ECG domain.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Dataset and Partitioning}

We use the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database \cite{b1}, which contains 48
half-hour two-lead ECG recordings sampled at 360\,Hz, with 100,682
annotated beats.  Following AAMI~EC57 \cite{b2} and the de~Chazal
inter-patient protocol \cite{b3}, records are split into a training
cohort (DS1) and a disjoint test cohort (DS2).  The class distribution
after partitioning is shown in Table~\ref{tab:split}.

\subsection{Pre-processing}

Each recording is processed as follows:
(1)~Baseline wander removal using a high-pass filter at 0.5\,Hz;
(2)~QRS complex detection with the Pan--Tompkins algorithm \cite{b7};
(3)~Fixed-length beat segmentation of 187 samples centred on the R-peak;
(4)~Extraction of two auxiliary RR-interval features---the pre-beat and
post-beat RR intervals, each normalised by the local 10-beat mean RR.

\subsection{Model Architecture}

Each of the five ensemble members is a 1D-CNN:

\begin{equation}
f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \text{Softmax}\bigl(W_2\,\sigma(W_1\,h(\mathbf{x}) + b_1) + b_2\bigr)
\end{equation}

where $h(\mathbf{x})$ denotes the convolutional feature extractor.
Concretely the network is:
\textit{Input}$(187{,}1)$ $\to$ Conv1D(32,5) $\to$ BN $\to$ ReLU $\to$
MaxPool(2) $\to$ Conv1D(64,3) $\to$ BN $\to$ ReLU $\to$ MaxPool(2) $\to$
Flatten $\to$ Concat(RR) $\to$ Dense(64) $\to$ Dropout(0.5) $\to$
Dense(32) $\to$ Dropout(0.5) $\to$ Dense(5, Softmax).

Training uses class-weighted categorical cross-entropy with weights
inversely proportional to class frequency, the Adam optimiser
(lr~$= 10^{-3}$) \cite{b8}, batch size 256, and early stopping
(patience~3, monitored on validation macro-F1).

\subsection{Ensemble and Calibration}

Five models are trained independently with seeds 0--4.
At inference the ensemble prediction is the mean of calibrated
per-member softmax outputs:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{ens}}(y|\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
  \text{Softmax}\!\left(\frac{z_m(\mathbf{x})}{T_m}\right),
\end{equation}
where $T_m$ is the per-member temperature optimised by minimising
negative log-likelihood on the validation set.
Learned temperatures are $T = [0.836, 1.183, 1.487, 0.979, 1.325]$.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}

Four uncertainty metrics are evaluated.

\textbf{Softmax confidence:}
$u_{\text{SC}} = 1 - \max_y p_{\text{ens}}(y|\mathbf{x})$.

\textbf{Predictive entropy (PE):}
$u_{\text{PE}} = -\sum_y p_{\text{ens}}\log p_{\text{ens}}$.

\textbf{MC-Dropout variance:}
20 stochastic forward passes with dropout enabled; variance averaged
over classes.

\textbf{Cluster-Based Entropy (CBE):}
A novel formulation that partitions the $M$ ensemble members into
clinical-risk clusters $\{\text{N}\}$, $\{\text{S}\}$,
$\{\text{V,F}\}$, $\{\text{Q}\}$ according to their
argmax predictions, then computes the Shannon entropy of the
resulting cluster-frequency distribution.  This captures high-risk
disagreement while suppressing benign intra-cluster variance, and
requires no additional forward passes.

\section{Results}

49,687 unseen DS2 test beats.  The ensemble improves accuracy from
79.29\% (single model) to \textbf{85.86\%} and macro-F1 from 0.329 to
\textbf{0.371}.  Normal and Ventricular beats are detected reliably
(F1 $= 0.924$ and $0.809$, respectively), while Supraventricular and
Fusion beats remain challenging due to extreme under-representation

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Ensemble benefit.}  The 6.6 percentage-point accuracy improvement
and 0.042 macro-F1 gain from single model to ensemble confirm that
diversity induced by random initialisation reduces generalisation error
under class imbalance.

\textbf{Minority class challenge.}  Despite class-weighted loss,
Supraventricular (F1 $= 0.098$) and Fusion (F1 $= 0.022$) beats remain
difficult.  Both classes are underrepresented: S constitutes only 3.7\%
and F only 0.8\% of the training set.  Additional data augmentation or
synthetic over-sampling (e.g., SMOTE) may improve these classes.

\textbf{Calibration.}  Temperature scaling provides a reliable,
post-training calibration step that reduces ECE without retraining.
The calibrated ensemble is better suited for threshold-based clinical
decision rules.

\textbf{Uncertainty.}  Softmax confidence, the simplest metric,
outperforms all others (AUROC $= 0.819$).  The proposed CBE achieves
AUROC $= 0.798$ with no additional forward passes, making it attractive
for resource-constrained deployment.

\textbf{Limitations.}
(1)~The Q class contains only 7 test beats, too few for meaningful
    evaluation.
(2)~External validation on independent databases (e.g., INCART, PTB-XL)
    is needed to confirm generalisability.
(3)~Only the MLII lead is used; multi-lead fusion may improve
    S and F class detection.

% ==================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

We presented a calibrated deep ensemble for ECG arrhythmia detection
evaluated under the rigorous inter-patient DS1/DS2 protocol.  On 49,687
unseen test beats the system achieves 85.86\% accuracy and 0.371
macro-F1, with post-hoc temperature scaling reducing ECE to 3.04\%.
A comprehensive uncertainty benchmark demonstrates that referring the
10\% most uncertain predictions to a clinician raises retained accuracy
to 89.6\%.  The proposed Cluster-Based Entropy metric matches predictive
entropy performance without requiring additional forward passes.  All
source code and trained model weights are publicly available at
\texttt{https://github.com/Bijon2002/ECG-Arrhythmia-Detection}.
